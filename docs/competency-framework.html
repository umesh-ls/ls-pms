LS PMS — Competency Framework V1

6 October 2026 · For the quarterly review system · Applies to all functions Six competencies, same for everyone. Two of them carry a definition that changes by function. One framework, not five.

The six

# Competency What it means Varies by function
1 Delivery & Accountability Delivers what was agreed, on the timeline agreed. Owns the outcome rather than the task. Raises problems early instead of at the deadline. No
2 Technical / Functional Skill Depth and currency in the craft the role is paid for. Yes
3 Quality of Work Whether the output was right. Rework, errors, things that come back. Yes
4 Problem Solving & Judgement Breaks a problem down, chooses a sensible path, knows when to decide and when to escalate. No
5 Communication & Collaboration Clear in writing and in person, with the team and outward to clients. Works well with people who are not like them. No
6 Learning & Growth Builds new capability deliberately. Applies what they learn to real work rather than collecting certificates. No

Two marked primary — Delivery & Accountability and Technical / Functional Skill carry double weight. Time management counting the same as technical competence was never what LS valued.

Dropped from the old seven: Result Orientedness and Time Management merge into Delivery & Accountability. Teamwork merges into Communication & Collaboration. Intrapreneurship Mindset is replaced by Learning & Growth — observable, and pointed at the capability gap LS actually has.

Added: Quality of Work. Nothing in the old seven asked whether the output was correct, which for a services business is the thing clients pay for and complain about.

The two that vary by function

Same competency, same scale, different description of what good looks like. HR maintains one page, not five frameworks.

Function Technical / Functional Skill means Quality of Work means
Engineering & Technology (77) Code, design and tooling in their stack; depth appropriate to grade Defects reaching QA or production, rework, incidents traced to their work
QA (within Engineering) Test design, automation, coverage Escaped defects, noise in reported bugs
Design (within Engineering) Craft, design systems, usability thinking Rework after handoff; whether the work was build-ready
Sales & Marketing (15) Pipeline management, proposal craft, domain knowledge Accuracy of proposals and forecasts; data hygiene in the CRM
Human Resources & Admin (8) Recruitment, policy, employee relations, systems Accuracy of records, letters and payroll inputs
Finance & Legal (5) Statutory knowledge, controls, systems Error rate, audit findings, corrections after issue
IT & Hardware (4) Infrastructure, support, security practice Repeat tickets, unresolved root causes

Why Quality of Work earns its place: three FY26 appraisal letters went out with the Other Allowance annual figure not equal to monthly × 12, and one promotion letter carried the wrong grade. Those were quality failures in a function where nothing on the old form would have captured them.

Rating anchors

Scale 1–5. A written example is mandatory at 4 and 5 — name the project, the deliverable and roughly when.

Delivery & Accountability Quality of Work Learning & Growth
2 — Needs improvement Commitments slip without warning; needs chasing Work regularly comes back; others check behind them No visible effort to build capability
3 — Meets expectations Delivers what was agreed; flags problems in time to act Output is sound; occasional corrections, caught early Keeps current with what the role requires
4 — Exceeds expectations Takes on more than the brief; unblocks others; nothing slips silently Others rely on their work being right first time Builds a capability the team did not have and uses it on real work

Levels 1 and 5 sit at the extremes of the same ladder. 5 is for a role model — the person you would point a new joiner at.

The remaining three competencies follow the same shape: 2 is inconsistent and needs support, 3 is reliably at standard, 4 is beyond the role, 5 is exemplary.

Why not a different set per function

  • Comparability. The increment matrix is org-wide. Five frameworks feeding one matrix produces incomparable inputs, and calibration across departments becomes impossible.
  • Proportion. 77 of 109 people sit in Engineering. Building and maintaining five frameworks for the other 32 is work out of all proportion to the benefit.
  • Maintenance. One framework with two function-variable definitions is a single page HR owns. Five frameworks drift apart within two cycles.

The variation that matters — what "skilled" and "correct" mean in a given job — is captured in the two definitions above without fracturing the framework.

Applying this to quarters already captured

Q1 (Apr–Jun) was collected on the old seven. Map it rather than discard it:

  • Result Orientedness + Time Management → average into Delivery & Accountability
  • Communication + Teamwork → average into Communication & Collaboration
  • Technical / Functional and Problem Solving → carry across unchanged
  • Intrapreneurship → does not map; leave out
  • Quality of Work has no Q1 source — Q1 scores on five of six, and the quarter still produces a rating

State the mapping on the Q1 record so nobody later mistakes it for a six-competency quarter.
